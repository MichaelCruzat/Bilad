# NASA Space Apps winner patterns

Scope: public product surfaces only. Primary sources are NASA/Space Apps winner and judging pages plus winner-owned sites or public repositories. “Observed” means stated or visibly presented by a source; “inference” means a qualitative interpretation, not a timed usability study.

## 1. Which projects won or were recognized, and what did the public product present?

### Takeaway

Across 2023–2025, the global winners repeatedly turn NASA data into a concrete user action, decision, or learning moment: forecast a storm, choose a planting window, receive a satellite-pass alert, inspect a 3D world, test a habitat under a live solar event, or expose a molecular risk. 2026 has no winners yet as of 2026-11-15; NASA’s published timeline places Global Winners in January 2027.

### Cited Findings

- 2023 results were announced by NASA Science on Jan. 26, 2024. The 10 Global Winners were selected from 5,556 submissions by NASA and 13 Space Agency Partner subject-matter experts; the challenge had 57,999 registered participants in 152 countries/territories. The winners were: LunarTech Ensemble (Best Use of Science), Storm Prophet (Best Use of Data), Spacebee (Best Use of Technology), Greetings from Earth!! (Galactic Impact), ASTROGENESIS (Best Mission Concept), Space Quest Maidens (Most Inspirational), TeamVoyagers (Best Storytelling), Arcobaleno (Global Connection), Oogway Comics (Art & Technology), and $quality_over_quantity (Local Impact). [NASA Science: 2023 Global Winners](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/)
- The 2023 software/data surfaces were varied but legible: LunarTech’s Lunar Trek paired an Apollo moonquake website with an Unreal Engine game; Storm Prophet used DSCOVR data and LSTM models to forecast geomagnetic storms; Spacebee plotted Apollo moonquake locations/types/dates; Greetings from Earth!! visualized NASA ocean data; ASTROGENESIS let users make personalized planetary itineraries; Arcobaleno sonified NASA imagery for blind users; and $quality_over_quantity mapped biodiversity hotspots. [NASA Science: 2023 descriptions](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/)
- 2024 results were announced by NASA on Jan. 16, 2025. The 10 winners were selected from 9,996 submissions; the challenge had 93,520 registrants across 163 countries/territories. The winners were WMPGang/SkyShield (Best Use of Science), GaamaRamma/WaterWise (Best Use of Data), 42 QuakeHeroes (Best Use of Technology), NVS-knot/2plant (Galactic Impact), AsturExplorers/Landsat Connect (Best Mission Concept), Innovisionaries/Eco-Metropolis (Most Inspirational), TerraTales (Best Storytelling), Asteroid Destroyer (Global Connection), Connected Earth Museum (Art & Technology), and Team I.O./G.R.O.W. (Local Impact). [NASA: 2024 Global Winners](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)
- The strongest 2024 product concepts are explicit: SkyShield identifies satellite-risk zones from real-time Near-Earth Object and meteor-stream data; WaterWise helps farmers manage water; 2plant assesses planting risk from soil moisture and evapotranspiration; Landsat Connect tracks satellites and alerts users when Landsat passes a target location; and G.R.O.W. makes complex GIS/environmental information usable to non-experts. [NASA: 2024 product summaries](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)
- 2025 results were announced by NASA on Dec. 18, 2025. The 10 winners were selected from more than 11,500 submissions; the challenge had more than 114,000 participants at 551 events in 167 countries/territories. The winners were SpaceGenes+ (Best Use of Science), Resonant Exoplanets (Best Use of Data), Twisters/SkySense (Best Use of Technology), Astro Sweepers (Galactic Impact), PureFlow (Best Mission Concept), Photonics Odyssey (Most Inspirational), HerCode Space (Best Storytelling), Gaia+LEO (Global Connection), Zumorroda-X (Art & Technology), and QUEÑARIS (Local Impact). [NASA: 2025 Global Winners](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/)
- 2025’s software/data winners push beyond “visualize NASA data”: SpaceGenes+ is a molecular-space-biology dashboard; Resonant Exoplanets analyzes telescope/satellite data for exoplanets and biosignatures; SkySense produces ultra-local weather risk and safest time windows; Astro Sweepers generates orbital-debris risk/compliance intelligence; PureFlow designs and validates habitats in 3D against space-weather threats; Gaia+LEO optimizes orbital/terrestrial data-center networks; and QUEÑARIS combines satellite data, drones, AI, and biotechnology for reforestation in Arequipa. [NASA: 2025 product summaries](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/)
- 2026 is current but not yet a winner-data source: NASA’s 2026 recruitment PDF schedules the challenge for Nov. 14–15, 2026, Global Nominees/Finalists in December, and Global Winners in January 2027. [NASA Space Apps 2026 timeline](https://assets.spaceappschallenge.org/media/documents/2026_NASA_Space_Apps_Registration_Recruitment.pdf)

### Inferences

- The public winners span dashboards, maps, models, games, stories, and simulations, but the common product shape is “complex NASA data in → recognizable user action out.”
- Live deployment, open code, or a clearly documented data/method chain is not universal, but the strongest public surfaces make at least one of those visible.

### Gaps

- NASA’s Space Apps pages sometimes failed to load directly in the research fetcher, and several deployed apps are JavaScript-only. I did not infer unobserved UI behavior from private judging materials or inaccessible screens.

## 2. How quickly can a visitor understand the problem, user, data, action, and proof?

### Takeaway

Qualitatively, the fastest surfaces make the user and action legible in the hero sentence, then place demo/proof links immediately after. A visitor can usually form a usable mental model in roughly 20–45 seconds for Storm Prophet, SpaceGenes+, Landsat Connect, Astro Sweepers, and Resonant Worlds Explorer; NVS/2plant is more like 1–2 minutes because its strongest evidence is buried in a long project narrative. These are inspection estimates, not measured task times.

### Cited Findings

- NASA’s published judging lenses are Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, and Presentation. The 2024 judging description says the global process reviews project demos/pages and that executive judges select winners; NASA’s 2025 awards page defines the award categories around valid science, unique use of data, innovative technology, plausible mission design, storytelling, connection, and local impact. [2024 judging description](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/2024-local-events/pangyo/); [2025 awards](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/)
- Storm Prophet’s public project page starts with “Predicting Geomagnetic Storms Using Data Analysis and LSTM Models,” then exposes a demonstration, GitHub project, live forecast, data scope, model, evaluation metric, and stated error rates. [Storm Prophet project page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/storm-prophet/?tab=project)
- AsturExplorers starts with the user-facing promise “track Landsat satellites and access Landsat Surface Reflectance data,” then states the first action—set a target location and receive notifications—before listing details, use cases, architecture, accessibility, and source links. [Landsat Connect](https://www.asturexplorers.space/landsat-connect-app/)
- SpaceGenes+ starts with the audience/problem (“interactive space bioinformatics dashboard” using NASA GeneLab data), names users (researchers, drug discovery teams, mission planners), shows a three-step input/analysis/output flow, prints its synergy formula, and then links to a live dashboard and NASA paper. [SpaceGenes+](https://spacegenes-plus.framer.website/)
- Resonant Worlds Explorer’s repo opens with a one-sentence product description, then gives a numbered capability list, runnable setup, API examples, scientific-validation section, metrics, deployment details, and live project link. [Resonant Worlds Explorer repo](https://github.com/KingTadar12/resonant-planet)
- NVS/2plant eventually makes the decision loop unusually concrete: planting sufficiency, equipment safety, risk scores, 100 m resolution, two-week forecast, uncertainty, and export/API options. The same page also includes long background sections on hydrology, product fit, market size, and pricing, which increases evidence but slows first comprehension. [NVS-knot project page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/nvs-knot/?tab=project)
- Lunar Trek’s public repo makes the interaction understandable through a memorable visual model—3D Moon pins plus time-series analysis—and names the NASA catalog, technologies, and game. [Lunar Trek repo](https://github.com/yousefkotp/Lunar-Trek)

### Inferences

- A fast first visit follows this sequence: “For [specific person], this turns [NASA dataset] into [one decision/experience], and here is the proof.” This is an inference from the repeated page order and content of the reviewed winners, not a NASA-published formula.
- The best public proof is adjacent to the claim: a live link or demo for interaction, a repo or data link for reproducibility, a formula/metric for validity, and a limitation note for trust.
- “Scientific credibility” is easier to perceive when the product names the source dataset/study, exposes the transformation or formula, and shows one inspectable result; SpaceGenes+, Storm Prophet, Resonant Worlds Explorer, and NVS/2plant all do some version of this.

### Gaps

- No public source reviewed provides judge-by-judge scoring or controlled first-time visitor timing. The speed bands above should guide design review, not be treated as benchmark data.

## 3. What interaction/presentation patterns recur, and what do strong winners avoid?

### Takeaway

Strong winners reduce the distance between data and consequence. They wrap expert datasets in familiar interaction patterns, make the “before/after” or “input/output” visible, and give a judge a short path from story to proof. They avoid leaving the visitor with only an impressive visualization, an ungrounded AI claim, or an unclear prototype/deployment status.

### Cited Findings

- Action-centered wrappers recur: a map plus alert (Landsat Connect), field polygon plus schedule (WaterWise/2plant), dataset plus heatmap (SpaceGenes+), raw observations plus forecast (Storm Prophet), orbital data plus risk/compliance score (Astro Sweepers), and habitat layout plus live solar-storm stress test (PureFlow). [NASA 2023 winners](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/); [NASA 2024 winners](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/); [NASA 2025 winners](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/)
- Familiar interfaces make specialist data approachable: 3D globes/orreries (Lunar Trek, SkyShield), conversational or voice interaction (G.R.O.W.), map pinning/alerts (Landsat Connect), dashboards/heatmaps (SpaceGenes+), and games/storytelling (TeamVoyagers, Eco-Metropolis, HerCode Space). The official winner descriptions repeatedly emphasize accessibility, education, or real-world decision support rather than raw data exposure. [NASA 2023 winners](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners/); [NASA 2024 winners](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/); [SpaceGenes+](https://spacegenes-plus.framer.website/); [Landsat Connect](https://www.asturexplorers.space/landsat-connect-app/)
- Layered presentation is common on winner-owned surfaces: one-sentence promise, demo/live link, detailed method, sources, and next steps. AsturExplorers and SpaceGenes+ are especially explicit; Resonant Worlds Explorer adds a public runbook and validation section. [AsturExplorers](https://www.asturexplorers.space/landsat-connect-app/); [SpaceGenes+](https://spacegenes-plus.framer.website/); [Resonant Worlds Explorer](https://github.com/KingTadar12/resonant-planet)
- Accessibility is not only a visual-style claim: Arcobaleno changes the sensory channel itself through sonification for blind users; AsturExplorers describes WCAG-oriented accessibility, keyboard navigation, contrast, localization, and multiple notification methods. [Arcobaleno project page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/arcobaleno/?tab=project); [Landsat Connect](https://www.asturexplorers.space/landsat-connect-app/)
- Public submissions are expected to be open and citable. NASA’s legal guidance says teams must cite every source used, including open-source resources, and that NASA may remove a solution or award status for missing citations. [NASA Space Apps legal guidance](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/)
- Strong surfaces avoid blurring “concept,” “prototype,” and “deployed product.” SpaceGenes+ explicitly says only one case is live and others are conceptual; AsturExplorers uses future-tense product language and external reporting indicates development was still being completed; Astro Sweepers explicitly says it is not NASA-endorsed. [SpaceGenes+](https://spacegenes-plus.framer.website/); [AsturExplorers](https://www.asturexplorers.space/landsat-connect-app/); [Astro Sweepers](https://astro-sweepers.vercel.app/pricing)
- A caution from public inspection: Astro Sweepers has strong product framing, pricing, and disclaimers, but the homepage’s “99.8% collision prediction accuracy” is not accompanied by a visible benchmark on the reviewed surface, and its contact page includes placeholder-looking phone/address values. This is exactly the kind of visible mismatch that can undercut otherwise strong presentation. [Astro Sweepers home](https://astro-sweepers.vercel.app/home); [Astro Sweepers contact](https://astro-sweepers.vercel.app/contact)

### Inferences

- Reusable winning pattern: **human stakes → one-sentence data transformation → one visible interaction → one inspectable proof artifact → honest status/limitation**.
- For a judge or first-time visitor, polish is most valuable when it helps answer five questions in order: Who is this for? What problem matters? Which NASA data is used? What can I do now? Why should I believe the result?
- Avoid: a generic “AI-powered platform” headline with no named user; raw charts without an action; a multi-feature tour before the core moment; metrics without a benchmark or source; NASA branding that implies endorsement; and future roadmap language presented as deployed capability.
- The best “wow” moments are consequential, not decorative: a forecast changes a risk state, a location triggers an alert, a simulation fails or passes under a real event, a model filters a false positive, or a dataset produces a result that can be exported/shared.

### Gaps

- The reviewed public surfaces do not prove long-term adoption, repeat users, production uptime, or scientific peer review for most winners. Treat deployment, validation, and impact claims as “visible evidence on the public surface,” not as independently confirmed outcomes.
